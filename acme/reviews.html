What cooks tell us

We don't run contests for reviews or hand out discount codes for five stars. These came in by email and on order pages, and we publish them the way people wrote them — the doubts included. A lot of folks arrive here unsure whether cast iron is worth the fuss, so we've grouped the reviews by the question people actually ask before they buy.

"Is it worth the price?"

★★★★★

I stared at the $95 for a week. I'd bought three "nonstick" pans in five years and thrown all three out when the coating flaked. Did the math and realized I'd already spent more than the Skillet costs, just in garbage. Two years in, this one looks better than the day it arrived. I'm annoyed I waited.

— Marcus T., Denver, CO

★★★★★

I run a small supper club out of my house and cook most nights. My old pans were a revolving door. The Dutch Oven has done braises, bread, and about forty batches of chili and hasn't blinked. Price per meal, it's already the cheapest thing in my kitchen.

— Priya R., Austin, TX

★★★★☆

Not cheap, and I won't pretend otherwise. What sold me was the lifetime guarantee — that's a company telling you it expects the thing to outlive the argument. Four stars only because I wish the Glass Lid came bundled. Everything else, no notes.

— Dana L., Portland, OR

"I'm scared of rust and ruining it"

★★★★★

Full confession: I was terrified. Every article online made cast iron sound like a houseplant that dies if you look at it wrong. Reality? I rinse it, wipe it dry, rub a thumbnail of oil in. That's the whole ritual. I left it wet once and got a faint orange haze; thirty seconds with the scrubber and it was gone. Nothing about this is fragile.

— Jenna K., Columbus, OH

★★★★★

My mother-in-law told me I'd wreck it. It got a little rust spot after a camping trip. I panicked and emailed Acme expecting a sales pitch. Instead a real person walked me through scrubbing it back and re-oiling. Fixed in ten minutes. The pan is genuinely un-killable, which is the opposite of what I feared.

— Ohemaa B., Silver Spring, MD

★★★★★

The Care Kit is the thing that calmed me down. The wax makes re-seasoning obvious instead of mysterious, and the chainmail scrubber takes the "am I allowed to scrub it" question off the table. Buy it with the pan and stop worrying.

— Tom V., Boise, ID

"Does it actually work on induction?"

★★★★★

Renter here, glass induction cooktop, no gas allowed. Cast iron is basically the perfect induction material — it's solid iron, so the burner locks onto it instantly and it holds heat like nothing else. Sears a steak better than the fancy pan my landlord left. Just lift it, don't slide it, so you don't scratch the glass.

— Sofia M., Brooklyn, NY

★★★★★

Switched to an induction range last year and had to rebuild my whole kitchen. The Skillet and Dutch Oven were the two things that worked on day one with zero adapters. Even heat, no hot ring in the middle. This is what induction was made for.

— Raj P., Fremont, CA

"Will it make a good gift?"

★★★★★

Bought the Skillet for my son's first apartment. He's twenty-three and owns almost nothing. The idea that this is the one pan he might still have at fifty got me a little emotional at the checkout, honestly. It arrived in a box that looked like a gift, not a shipment.

— Carol S., Naperville, IL

★★★★★

Gave the Dutch Oven to my daughter and son-in-law for the holidays. My own cast iron came from my grandmother and I cook with it every week. Handing down a tool instead of a gadget felt right. They've already made bread in it twice.

— William O., Chattanooga, TN

"It's heavy and I do the maintenance thing"

★★★★☆

Yes, it's heavy — that's the honest tradeoff and nobody should pretend otherwise. But the weight is why it sits still, holds heat, and sears. I use two hands for the Dutch Oven when it's full. If you have wrist trouble, size down to the Skillet. The heft is the feature; it just takes a week to get used to.

— Eleanor F., Santa Fe, NM

★★★★★

I bought recycled iron partly to feel less guilty about buying more stuff, and honestly assumed the "recycled" bit was marketing. Then it just... kept being the only pan I reach for. If one pan replaces the four I'd have cycled through, that's the sustainability story, and it's a real one. Warm to use, built to last, made from metal that already existed. Sold.

— Nadia H., Minneapolis, MN
