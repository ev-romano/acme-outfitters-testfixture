The one-pan week: dinners from a single skillet

From the bench

The people we build for tend to keep their tools for twenty years and cook most nights at home. They're not chasing gadgets; they want a small kit that works hard. So here's a challenge we love: a full week of weeknight dinners out of one 10-inch Skillet. No specialized pan for eggs, no separate roasting tray, no third pot for sauce. One pan, hot, going from stovetop to oven and back. Fewer dishes, and — this is the part people don't expect — better food, because cast iron sears and holds heat in a way thin pans can't.

Monday — seared chicken thighs

Skin-side down in a screaming-hot Skillet, undisturbed, until the fat renders and the skin is glass-crisp. Flip, slide the whole pan into the oven to finish. The pan that seared the chicken is the pan that roasts it — that's the trick cast iron makes easy, because it's as happy at 450°F in the oven as it is on the burner. Rest the thighs, and use the fond left in the pan for a two-minute sauce.

Tuesday — a proper weeknight steak

You don't need a grill. A hot cast iron Skillet gives a steak a better crust than most grills do, because the whole surface is in contact with the meat. Salt, hot pan, a knob of butter at the end, baste, done. This is the meal that converts skeptics — the first time someone gets a steakhouse crust on a Tuesday in their own kitchen, the pan has paid for itself in their head.

Wednesday — the one-pan vegetable dinner

Roast a pan of chopped vegetables — potatoes, onions, whatever's wilting in the drawer — at high heat until the edges caramelize. Cast iron's heat retention is what browns them instead of steaming them. Crack a few eggs into the gaps near the end, cover with the Glass Lid so you can watch the whites set, and you've got dinner without a second dish to wash.

Thursday — fish, gently

People assume cast iron is only for high-heat brute force. Not so. A well-seasoned pan is genuinely nonstick for a fillet of fish, skin-side down, over medium heat. The seasoning you've built up all week — every steak, every chicken thigh added to it — is exactly what lets the fish release clean. This is seasoning working for you.

Friday — skillet pizza or a cornbread

End the week baking. Press a dough into the Skillet, top it, and bake — the preheated iron gives you a crackly bottom crust no baking sheet can match. Not in a pizza mood? Same pan, a batter, and you've got a golden-edged cornbread. From raw-meat sear on Monday to bread on Friday, one pan did all of it.

Why this works

The one-pan week isn't a stunt. It's the whole argument for how we think about cookware. Most kitchens are crowded with single-use pans that each get pulled out a few times a year. A cast iron Skillet collapses most of that into one object you actually reach for every night — and the more you use it, the better it cooks, because the seasoning deepens with every meal. That's the opposite of a nonstick pan, which is best on day one and downhill from there.

Buy one thing, use it hard, keep it for decades. That's not a diet of deprivation. Cooked right, it's a better week of dinners with half the cleanup. If you're setting up that one pan, the care routine takes about a minute a night, and it's what turns a good pan into a great one over the years.
