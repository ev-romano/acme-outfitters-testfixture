What's in it

Two things, and only the two things that actually matter for keeping cast iron alive:

  • Seasoning wax. A solid flaxseed-oil-based wax — the same oil we season with at the foundry. A pea-sized amount buffed onto a warm pan builds and refreshes the seasoning layer. It's less messy than pouring oil from a bottle and it's the exact stuff, not a mystery blend.
  • Chainmail scrubber. A palm-sized ring of stainless-steel chainmail. It lifts stuck-on food and light rust without stripping the seasoning underneath — which is the thing a scouring pad or steel wool gets wrong.

When to use each

The scrubber comes out when there's stuck food or the surface feels rough. Rinse the pan under hot water, scrub in a few circles, and food lifts off without soap or soaking. If a pan has picked up a little orange rust from sitting wet, the chainmail knocks it back to bare metal in under a minute.

The wax comes out after cleaning, or whenever the pan starts looking dull or gray instead of glossy black. Warm the dry pan slightly, buff in a tiny amount of wax with a cloth, then wipe it back so it looks almost dry. That thin film is what keeps the surface non-stick and rust-free.

"But isn't cast-iron maintenance hard?"

This kit exists precisely because that fear keeps people away from cast iron, and it's mostly a myth. The whole routine after cooking is: wipe, rinse hot, dry, wipe with a little oil or wax. Thirty seconds. The scrubber handles the one genuinely annoying case — stuck food — and the wax handles the other — a tired-looking surface. With these two in the drawer there's no soaking, no scouring, no scrubbing a pan raw, and no reason to be afraid of rust. If you're gifting a skillet to someone new to cast iron, add this so their first month is easy.
