10-inch or 12-inch?

Most people should start with the 10-inch. It's lighter (about 4.5 lb), fits every burner, and handles dinner for two to four. Choose the 12-inch if you regularly cook for a family, like to sear a lot of food in one go, or already own the 10-inch and keep wishing it were bigger. The 12-inch needs a large burner to heat evenly; on a small burner, preheat it longer, about 8 minutes on medium.

The usual worries

Weight. About 7 lb empty. That's the honest tradeoff for a pan this size. If you have wrist or grip trouble, the 10-inch is the better choice, and we'll say so every time.

Induction and glass tops. Works on induction, gas, electric and ceramic. Lift, don't slide, on glass tops.

Rust. Same as every pan we make: dry it, oil it, and it won't rust. If it does, it's fixable. See care.
