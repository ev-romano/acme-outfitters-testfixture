Care for gear that gets dirty

Camp cookware should show use. Care is not about keeping it showroom-new; it is about keeping water, rust, grit, and old food from shortening its useful life.

Carbon-steel seasoning

The Camp Skillet arrives pre-seasoned with grapeseed oil and beeswax. Before its first meal, rinse it, dry it over low heat, and add a thin coat of cooking oil. For routine cleaning, scrape away food while the pan is warm, rinse with hot water, and use a little mild soap when needed. Modern soap will not undo sound seasoning. Avoid the dishwasher and long soaks.

Dry the skillet completely over low heat. While it is warm, wipe on a tiny amount of neutral cooking oil, then wipe again as though you made a mistake. Thick oil turns sticky. Thin layers, heat, and repeated meals build the useful dark patina.

Rust rescue

  1. Scrub orange rust with an abrasive pad, coarse salt, or fine steel wool until clean steel shows.
  2. Rinse and dry immediately over low heat.
  3. Rub on a whisper-thin coat of neutral oil.
  4. Heat until the oil bonds and the surface looks dry, then cool safely.
  5. Repeat a thin coat if the repaired area still looks bare.

Rust and a patchy season are fixable. If you would rather hand the job to us, we reseason the skillet free. You ship it to the Hood River barn; we ship it back. Contact us before mailing it.

Enamel mugs and plates

Our enamelware is dishwasher safe, though hand washing with a soft sponge is gentler. Soak cooked-on food and use a non-scratch pad. Do not attack the glass enamel with steel wool. The mugs may go on a stove or fire only when not empty. Plates are serving pieces, not cookware, and should stay off direct flame.

Enamel can chip after a drop or impact, especially on rock. Stop using a piece if a chip leaves a sharp edge or exposes loose glass. Impact chips are wear, not manufacturing defects.

Waxed canvas

Brush dirt from the empty utensil roll and spot-clean with cool water. Do not machine wash, dry clean, or tumble dry it. To re-wax at home, warm clean, dry canvas gently, rub in canvas wax, work it evenly with your fingers, and let it cure in a warm, ventilated place. Keep wax away from flame. Creases and color shifts are the material’s patina.

We also re-wax canvas rolls free for life. Send the empty roll in and we cover return shipping.

Kettle soot and other black marks

The Trail Kettle’s 18/8 stainless base will darken over fire. Soot is cosmetic. Wipe loose carbon outside, then wash with dish soap. A baking-soda paste can handle stubborn areas, but aggressive scouring only trades soot for scratches. Dry the kettle open before storage. Brush ash from the Folding Fire Grate, dry it, and oil bare steel lightly. Pack every piece only when it is cold and dry.
